\begin{abstract}
Real-world images are full of variance and interventions. For any image processing model these variations pose a hard challenge to solve as their performance deteriorates drastically under visual intervention, domain shifts and unseen classes. This report investigates such variations across four connected studies. First, it evaluated ResNet, ViT and CLIP models on STL-10 under various interventions like colour changes, translations, patch shuffling and cue conflicts. Model performance remained stable under colour and translation changes, however, disrupting spatial structure of images reduces their accuracy by 6.2--13.8 percentage points.
On cue conflicts, strong shape bias has been observed across all models, while texture also had an influence but not strong enough to change the final decisions. Second, unsupervised domain adaptation is analysed on the PACS dataset, with Sketch as the target domain. A 38.61 point macro-F1 domain gap is observed on source-only ERM. DAN and CDAN improved target recognition; however, class-specific negative transfer is also visible, whereas DANN suffered from optimisation instability. Third, it examined target-free domain generalisation on sketches using DAN-DG and SAM. DAN-DG exhibited the best Sketch macro-F1 of 72.37\% exceeding the target-aware DAN, however with stronger alignment push collapsed class-discriminative structure. SAM, in contrast, produced the lowest local sharpness proxy but failed to achieve better target performance, showing that local flatness of loss alone cannot achieve domain generalisation. Finally, the study examined open-set recognition on CIFAR-10. It demonstrated that semantically near unknowns are harder to reject than far ones. Among MSP, MLS, Energy and Mahalanobis, Mahalanobis distance performed the best on far unknowns. GCSC provided modest overall generalisation whereas PROSER offered limited meaningful rejections, given the experiment settings. In a nutshell, the observations show that domain invariance, confidence and local stability are not sufficient individually for reliable generalisation, as generalisation also depends on preserving the core semantic class structure, stable optimisation and the nature of the domain shift.

\section*{Introduction and Research Questions}
Modern vision models show strong performance under closed-set evaluation; however, they tend to struggle with real-world inputs because of the inherent complexity of real-world data. This report examines how different model architectures and experimental settings affect performance when input images differ in appearance, domain, or class composition. It evaluates robustness to visual interventions, domain adaptation, domain generalization, and unknown-class detection. It also investigates which features models rely on and whether alignment and local stability improve predictions on unseen domains and classes.

\section{Conclusion}
Across the experiments, it is observed that a strong closed-set performance did not guarantee better generalization and robustness under the distribution shift or unknown classes. Successful generalization required preserving the semantic information. Moderate alignment and augmentation helped, while excessive alignment led to unstable optimization and caused negative transfer. Overall, domain invariance, confidence or local flatness were not able to predict reliably on the target domain, highlighting the need for multiple complementary experiments and evaluations beyond IID accuracy.
